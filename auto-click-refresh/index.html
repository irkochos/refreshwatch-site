Auto click Chrome extension: refresh, then click

Some pages only show what you're waiting for after you press a button. RefreshWatch reloads the tab on a timer and, after every reload, clicks up to five buttons you pick, such as “Next month” and then “Search” on a booking page. It's free, with no account.

Updated October 26, 2026

What it does, and what it doesn't

RefreshWatch clicks buttons on the page in the order you set, once each, after every reload. Each click can wait 0 to 10 seconds first, and can wait up to 10 seconds for its button to appear. All waits together can be 60 seconds at most.

It isn't a macro recorder and it isn't a fast clicker. It doesn't record your mouse, type into forms, click at screen positions or click many times a second. It presses the buttons you picked, then reads the page.

Set up auto refresh and click in three steps

  1. Open the page and click the RefreshWatch icon. Under Refresh every, pick how often to reload. One to five minutes suits most pages.
  2. Open More options and turn on Click after reload. Click Pick, then click the button on the page. Choose + Add a click for the next button, up to five. For each click, set how long to wait before clicking, and tick Wait for it to appear for a button that loads late.
  3. Under When to click, choose Before reading the page if the clicks bring up what you're watching, or After reading the page. Click Start. Chrome asks to let RefreshWatch use that one site; allow it.

Example: “Next month”, then “Search”

A booking page opens on this month, and the free dates are in the next one. Set it up like this:

  • Click 1: the “Next month” button, no wait.
  • Click 2: the “Search” button, with Wait for it to appear ticked.
  • When to click: Before reading the page, so the check sees next month's results.
  • Watch for changes: Keyword, alert when “No appointments available” disappears.

The appointment slot alert guide walks through this setup with the cautions that come with booking sites.

Other things to click after a reload

  • A “Load more” or “Show all” button, so the full list is on the page before it's checked.
  • A “Check availability” or “Show times” button on a ticket or shop page.
  • A tab or filter on the page, such as your size, before a restock alert reads it.

You don't have to watch anything. With Watch for changes set to Off, RefreshWatch just reloads and clicks. To be told when something changes, see the website change alert guide.

When a click doesn't work

  • If a button isn't on the page after a reload, that click is skipped and the next ones still run. The popup says which one, for example “Click 2 of 3 wasn't found on the last refresh.” Pick it again if the page changed.
  • Clicks stay on the page. If a click opens a new page, RefreshWatch reads that page on the next refresh. Choose After reading the page, or pick a button that stays on the page.

Good to know

Clicking runs while Chrome is open and the tab stays open. It doesn't run in the cloud.

Some pages ignore clicks that don't come from a real mouse, or ask you to solve a check. RefreshWatch doesn't solve captchas, fill in forms or sign you in.

Chrome slows down tabs that have been in the background for more than 5 minutes, so a long click sequence there can run late. A check that takes longer than two minutes is stopped and tried again on the next refresh.

Automated clicking can be against a site's terms. Read them, and keep a few minutes between reloads.

Questions

Is RefreshWatch an auto clicker?

Not the kind that clicks many times a second. It clicks up to five buttons you pick, once each, after every reload of the page. It doesn't record macros or click at screen positions.

Can it click without reloading the page?

No. The clicks run as part of each refresh: RefreshWatch reloads the tab, then clicks the buttons you picked. Set how often with Refresh every, from 1 second to 24 hours.

How many buttons can it click?

Up to five per reload, in order. Each can wait 0 to 10 seconds before clicking and up to 10 seconds for its button to appear, with 60 seconds for all waits together.

Does it work on every site?

Not everywhere. Chrome doesn't let extensions use chrome:// pages or the Chrome Web Store, and a few sites ignore clicks that don't come from a real mouse. Clicking needs access to the site, and Chrome asks you for that one site.

Is it free?

Yes. RefreshWatch is free, with no paid tier and no account. It runs in your browser and sends nothing to us.
