A free tab reloader for Chrome

Chrome has no setting that reloads a tab on a timer. RefreshWatch adds one to every tab: pick an interval, press Start, and Chrome auto reloads the page until you stop it. Each tab keeps its own timer.

Updated October 26, 2026

Auto reload a page in three steps

  1. Add RefreshWatch to Chrome and pin it from the puzzle-piece menu. Open the page you want to reload and click the icon.
  2. Under Refresh every, pick 5 s, 10 s, 30 s, 1 min, 5 min or 15 min. Custom takes any time from 1 second to 24 hours, and Random picks a new time between two values for each reload.
  3. Click Start. The icon's badge counts down to the next reload, and the popup shows the countdown and how many refreshes have run. Click Stop when you're done.

Plain reloading doesn't ask for access to the site. Chrome lets an extension reload a tab without reading it.

Reload several tabs, each on its own timer

Start RefreshWatch in as many tabs as you like. Each tab has its own interval and countdown: a dashboard every 30 seconds in one tab, a job board every 15 minutes in another. Tabs keep reloading in the background, so the page doesn't have to be in front. There is no button that reloads every open tab at once; you start each tab you want.

Options for the timer

Open More options in the popup for these:

  • Stop after a number of refreshes, or Stop at a time of day.
  • Hard refresh (skip the cache) for a page that keeps showing an old copy.
  • Only refresh when I'm not on this tab, so a page never reloads while you read or type in it.
  • Keep scroll position, so a long page comes back where you were. This one needs access to the site.

Reload a page automatically whenever you open it

Choose Save as rule for this site. Next time you open a matching address, the timer starts by itself, even after Chrome restarts. A rule can match an exact address, every address that starts with one, or a wildcard such as https://*.example.com/tickets/*. Edit or turn off rules from Open rules & log.

More than reloading

  • Get told when the page changes, or when a word appears or disappears: website change alerts.
  • Click a button, such as “Load more”, after every reload: auto refresh and click.
  • Send the alert to your phone: phone alerts.

Good to know

Reloading runs while Chrome is open and your computer is awake. Closing the tab stops its timer.

Without access to the site, RefreshWatch can't tell when you go to another site in that tab, so it keeps reloading it until you stop it.

Reloading a site every few seconds for hours is heavy on the site and on your battery, and some sites block or slow down fast reloads. Use seconds for short bursts and respect each site's terms.

Questions

Can Chrome auto refresh a page without an extension?

No. Chrome has no built-in setting that reloads a tab on a timer. An extension such as RefreshWatch adds one, with a timer per tab.

Can I reload different tabs at different intervals?

Yes. Each tab has its own timer, so one tab can reload every 10 seconds while another reloads every 15 minutes.

Does it keep reloading when the tab is in the background?

Yes. The tab doesn't have to be in front. Chrome has to be running and your computer awake, and closing the tab stops its timer.

What is the shortest and longest interval?

From 1 second to 24 hours with Custom, or a random time between two values with Random.

Is it free?

Yes. RefreshWatch is free, with no paid tier and no account, and it doesn't send your browsing anywhere.
